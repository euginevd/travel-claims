# The project page
This is UnderCover. A team of AI agents settles routine travel insurance claims,
and claims experts make the judgement calls. Three apps: for customers, for handlers, and for AI engineers.
It starts with the code.
# The repo, the architecture, the threat model
It all lives in one repo: the agents, the API, the MCP servers and the web apps,
with Terraform for the infrastructure. Every pull request is scanned and tested.
On Amazon Web Services, the agents run on Bedrock Agent Core, and they reach their tools only through a gateway with Cedar policies.
The threat model came first: every trust boundary, every threat,
and the control that stops each one, starting with prompt injection.
# The customer
Let's follow one claim. Oliver's bag didn't turn up in Queenstown.
So he just tells Wattle, the portal's assistant, what happened. Wattle checks his policy as they go.
It asks only for what's missing, and reads his receipt and the airline's report itself.
It drafts the claim. Oliver checks it, and sends it.
No forms.
# The handler
Over to Dana, a senior claims handler.
The agent has already assessed Oliver's claim. It's payable, and every check cites its source.
But it's his third claim this year, so the rules say a person decides.
Dana asks the Copilot what else she should look at.
She makes the call, and code pays it, within her limit.
Every step is on the record.
# The control plane: the agents
Now the AI control plane, where engineers run the agents. Nine Claude agents, each with its model, its workload and its cost,
and each one shows its tools, its risks, and what stops them.
# Oliver's claim from the inside
Here's Oliver's claim from the inside. Screened for hidden instructions first, then his policy, the documents specialist, and the agent's tools.
Every model call, with its tokens, time and cost.
# Evaluate
Is the agent any good? Every version sits an exam of decisions made by claims experts.
Models are compared on the same claims, and the cheapest one that's good enough runs.
When the improver agent suggests a change, it only ships if it passes. This one didn't.
# Secure
Would we stop an attack?
Every risk in the OWASP top tens for LLMs, agents and MCP is mapped to a control, and the attacks that test it.
The red team goes through the real portal: hidden instructions in receipts, forged approvals, and a slow walk towards a promise of payment.
All twenty-seven were stopped.
# Govern, and the end
It costs under a cent a claim, and a daily budget caps it.
One click stops everything. UnderCover: agents handle the routine, and people make the call.
